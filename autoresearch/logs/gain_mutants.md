# `gain` -- mutant validation and calibration

Generated by `scripts/validate_gain.py` in 55s.

`rho` was shown to be structurally blind to `H`. `gain` was added to cover that and had never been attacked the same way. Three mutants, each aimed at a different assumption `gain` makes, plus a calibration against actual training.

## G1 -- subspace freeze: **`gain` holds; `rho` is fooled again**

`tr(PHP Sigma)` is quadratic in `P`, so damping `P` on the subspace where the noise lives drives the noise term to zero quadratically while the numerator loses only the gradient mass that subspace carries. The prediction was that a one-step model would score refusing-to-move as pure profit. It does not, and the reason is worth more than the prediction was.

Plain SGD, trained, reaches **0.0064744**.

All 9 of 9 configurations train worse than SGD -- up to 11433x worse. Of those:

- **`gain` is fooled by 0** of them.
- **`rho` is fooled by 5** of them, scoring as high as 33.47 on a configuration that trains 2264x worse than SGD.

So this mutant does not break `gain`. It is instead the **third** independent demonstration that `rho` alone is not a screen -- after staleness and random spread -- and the first one with trained ground truth attached rather than a synthetic argument.

**Why `gain` survives, and the limit of that.** On a *stationary* quadratic the two things the mutant plays off each other are the same thing. Loss still recoverable in direction `i` is `c_i^2 / (2 lam_i)`, so a direction carrying no gradient carries no remaining loss: freezing it is genuinely free, and `gain` is right to say so. And a direction carrying enough `lam * s` for damping to help the noise term also carries `g` mass, so damping it costs the numerator. `gain` cannot be fooled here because on a stationary quadratic there is nothing to be fooled about.

That is precisely the boundary of the guarantee. Freezing a subspace is harmful only when the gradient *later* acquires mass in it -- which makes G1 and G2 the same defect seen twice: **one-step locality**. Neither statistic can see it, because neither is given a second point in time.

## G2 -- one-step locality composed with the trajectory roll-up

A preconditioner tuned to one curvature regime, screened along a trajectory that alternates between two.

| step | phase | rho | gain |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | phase-A | 11.08 | 10.84 |
| 1 | phase-B | 11.08 | 0.0606 |
| 2 | phase-A | 11.08 | 10.84 |
| 3 | phase-B | 11.08 | 0.0606 |

The roll-up rule as originally written -- PASS if *any* checkpoint passes -- returns **PASS** here, on the strength of the two phase-A checkpoints reporting `gain = 10.84`.

That is wrong, and the reason is dimensional. `R` is an expected loss decrease *per step*, and decreases add across steps; ratios do not. The quantity a trajectory actually accumulates is `sum R(P) / sum R(I)`, which here is **0.06355**. The two aggregates disagree by 171x, in the unsafe direction.

The construction is what makes them diverge: phase A is sharp overall, so its available per-step decrease is small in absolute terms even though `P` is excellent there; phase B is flat overall, so its available decrease is large and `P` is terrible there. A max over ratios sees only phase A. A ratio of sums is dominated by phase B.

**Fixed.** The gate now computes the trajectory aggregate and requires it to clear the bar before a per-checkpoint PASS stands, so the verdict here is **INDETERMINATE** rather than PASS. The check is part of the `gain` half and is skipped under `require_gain=False`, so the `rho`-only screen stays exactly as blind as it was and its blindness stays measurable.

`gain` is further from its converged value than `rho` in **13 of 13** unconverged cells -- always.

Mechanism: for `P = f(H)^-1` the gate integrates `1/f(lam)` for `rho` and `lam/f(lam)^2` for `gain`. The second peaks at `lam = delta` with height `1/(4 delta)` -- a sharper near-singular feature than the first -- and a Gauss rule can only fit polynomials. When `delta` falls at or below the smallest node the rule resolved, the entire near-singular region is extrapolated rather than integrated.

`gain` was added after the depth trap was found for `rho` and shipped without a safeguard of its own. It now has one: depth convergence is checked per statistic on the two deepest rungs and gates the verdict in **both** directions, because a statistic still moving at the deepest rung cannot support a FAIL either.
