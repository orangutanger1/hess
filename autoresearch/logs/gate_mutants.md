**The two statistics move in opposite directions as the spread grows.** `rho` rises and `gain` falls, on a preconditioner that by construction knows nothing.  `rho` rewards per-coordinate spread for a structural reason: it contains `(g'Pg)^2 / ||g||^4`, which grows quadratically in `P`'s action on the gradient's support, while the compensating trace ratio `tr(P^-1 Sigma) / tr(P Sigma)` only recovers part of it -- an arithmetic-versus-harmonic asymmetry, not a sampling artifact.  `gain` is not fooled, because scaling directions up without regard to their curvature raises `g'PHPg` and `tr(PHP Sigma)` in the denominator.

This is the second independent demonstration on this page that the `rho` screen alone is insufficient -- the first being staleness.  The two failures are unrelated in mechanism and share one cause: `H` does not appear in `rho`.

## Why the gate is not `rho` alone

`H` does not appear in `rho`.  With `g` and `Sigma` held fixed while the curvature drifts, `rho` of a frozen preconditioner is therefore constant *by construction* -- it cannot detect staleness, at any sample size.

- frozen `P = H_0^-1`, `rho` at t=0: **11.0837**
- the same `P`, `rho` at t=T after the drift: **11.0837** (identical to machine precision: True)
- the same `P`, `gain` at t=T: **0.01119**

`rho`-only verdict: **PASS**.  Composite verdict: **FAIL**.

That gap is the same failure this project has already shipped once: a maximally-stale frozen basis scoring better than the correct implementation on both gates written to catch it.
